# Bóveda protocol

Six rules. One page. The moral perimeter for everything built under
this roof. Re-read every quarter. Sits with `cluster-architecture.md`,
`computational-orisha.md`, and `node-logic.md` as the editorial floor.

If a feature can't be built without breaking one of these rules, the
feature doesn't get built.

## 1. Cultural editorial power

The corpus is informed by Lucumí, Yoruba, Akan, Igbo, Egyptian, Vodou,
Rastafari, Maroon, Taíno traditions. The corpus does not get to
expand without consultation with practitioners and scholars from those
traditions. Their input is editorial, not advisory: they can veto
inclusions, request removals, and shape framing.

Concretely: each tradition has at least one named cultural advisor
with a documented relationship (paid, equity-bearing, or both).
Advisors are credited publicly. Inclusions made before this protocol
existed are subject to review.

## 2. Provenance tags everywhere

Every output produced by an espíritu, twin, or any non-human entity
carries a visible source tag. Ai-8O outputs are always tagged "Ai-8O
speaking" and never confused with the human Ubani. Cross-published
content (to Slayt, to public surfaces) carries the manifest signature
naming the entity that authored it.

No anonymous AI generation. No outputs that could be confused with a
human's voice unless the human has explicitly delegated that voice
for that act.

## 3. Royalty back upstream

A defined percentage of revenue earned through Bóveda routes back to
the cultural communities whose vocabulary, cosmology, and naming
practices the system uses. Routing is structural, not symbolic:
written into the Imperium settlement layer.

Initial proposal: 5% of net revenue distributed across cultural
advisor recipients and named community organisations. Reviewed
annually with advisors.

## 4. Consent for every voice and likeness

No voice training without signed consent from the speaker. No LoRA
training on a face without the person's go-ahead. This applies to
the dead: survivors decide. This applies to public figures: their
estates or successors decide. This applies to the user: every twin
or voice clone has documented consent on file.

Consent is revocable. When revoked, the artefact (voice file, LoRA
weights) is deleted, not just hidden.

## 5. Right to forget

Memory is tended, not accumulated automatically. Each entry in an
espíritu's eventLog carries a retention flag: ephemeral (default,
auto-prunes after 90 days) or canonical (kept indefinitely until the
user marks otherwise).

The user is custodian of the record. They can:
- Delete any single event
- Promote ephemeral events to canonical
- Demote canonical events to ephemeral
- Crystallise periods (compress a span into a summary)

This is not amnesia. It is curation. A bóveda altar is curated;
ancestors are remembered specifically, not exhaustively.

## 6. Posthumous protocol + open engine

Every user pre-declares what happens to their twin and their
espíritus when they die: continue, archive, dissolve, or transfer to
a named successor. Default is archive (the entity stops acting; the
record remains available to descendants by request).

The naming engine (@violet-sphinx/names) ships as a shared library.
Other diasporic creators can build their own Bóvedas, fork the
corpus, contribute back. The work is not a monopoly. The renaissance
is not a brand.

---

## Operating principle

Build the work first. Test the rules against real cases. Refine the
protocol from lived knowledge, not theory. Manifestos that run ahead
of the practice are how interesting projects die.

When in doubt, ask: would an initiate of one of these traditions
recognise what I am building as honouring or extracting? If the
answer is unclear, stop, consult, then proceed.
