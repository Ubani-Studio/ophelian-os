# Boveda Mappings — Research

*Working notes toward a book on mapping.*
*Companion to `Boveda Mapping Manifesto.md`.*

---

## 0. Scope

This document is a research spine. The manifesto states a position; this is the evidence, history, and taxonomy supporting it. The thesis of the book this is scaffolding toward is:

> **The act of mapping is never neutral. The dominant map of creative work in the modern West is descriptive-typological. A different compound — mythopoetic-operational mapping on a cosmological ground — is older, stranger, and more generative. Every major decolonial artist runs it. It deserves to be named, taught, and extended.**

---

## 1. What is a map

A map is a second-order object. It is not the world; it is a system of marks that lets the world be navigated, remembered, compared, changed.

Three properties of every map:

1. **Selection.** A map shows some things and hides others. What it hides is not an accident.
2. **Projection.** A map flattens dimensions onto fewer dimensions. Projection choices are ideological.
3. **Grammar.** A map names primitives (city, road, contour line) and operations (connect, enclose, measure). Different grammars produce different worlds.

When we talk about **mapping creative work** — how albums, films, ideas, careers, traditions are organized and named — we are choosing a grammar. Most people do not realize they are choosing.

## 2. Taxonomy of Mapping Modes

The modes below are not mutually exclusive. Real maps compound them. But each mode has a primary centre of gravity.

### 2.1 The modes

| Mode | Core operation | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Descriptive-typological** | Classifies what exists into nested types | Linnaean taxonomy; music-genre hierarchies; "rock → punk → hardcore" |
| **Symbolic** | Each sign points to another thing it is not | Tarot, heraldry, alchemy, church iconography |
| **Iconic** | Sign resembles referent | Realist painting, documentary photography |
| **Indexical** | Sign caused by or contiguous with referent | Footprint, shadow, thermometer |
| **Allegorical** | Systematic one-to-one correspondence between narratives | Dante's Commedia, Bunyan's Pilgrim's Progress, Spenser's Faerie Queene |
| **Analogical** | Structural similarity between domains | "The body politic"; organism as economy |
| **Cosmological** | Placement in a total world-order | Ifá's Odù, Kabbalah's Tree of Life, I Ching's hexagrams, Chinese five elements |
| **Ritual / Performative** | Meaning enacted through acts | Liturgy, oath-taking, invocation, *ashe* pronouncement |
| **Mythopoetic** | Author creates new world and names for it | Sun Ra; Tolkien; Le Guin; Octavia Butler; Wilson Harris |
| **Operational / Generative** | Meaning defined by what it generates | Pärt's tintinnabuli; code; Boveda XOR; Markov chains |
| **Apophatic** | Meaning by negation, by what the thing is not | Negative theology, Pseudo-Dionysius, Blanchot, some Zen |
| **Tidalectic / call-response** | Meaning between works, not in them | Brathwaite; Moten's "the break"; Morrison; oral traditions |

### 2.2 The key distinctions

**Typological vs. generative.** Typological maps sort a fixed world. Generative maps produce new instances. The Western default is typological; every major decolonial thinker is generative.

**Describing vs. authoring.** Descriptive maps presume the world is already named; they catalogue. Authoring maps treat naming as the creative act itself. Saying *opacity* instead of *obscurity* changes what can be done with the concept.

**Inherited vs. invented.** Inherited maps borrow substrate from a tradition (Ifá, Taoism). Invented maps coin everything from scratch. Pure invention tends toward abstraction; pure inheritance tends toward museum-work. Compound works best.

## 3. A brief history of mapping

### 3.1 The Western descriptive-typological line

- **Aristotle** — categories and substance. Every thing has an essence, a genus, a species. Types nest.
- **Linnaeus (18th c.)** — binomial nomenclature. Kingdom → phylum → class → order → family → genus → species. The template for every modern taxonomy.
- **Encyclopedists (Diderot, d'Alembert)** — all knowledge as tree.
- **Systematic musicology (19th–20th c.)** — genre classification, musical "families."
- **Music industry (20th c.)** — Billboard genre charts, retail bins, streaming taxonomy. The descriptive-typological map as commercial infrastructure.

The line's strength: legibility, comparability, search. Its weakness: cannot hold what does not fit. Cannot generate. Cannot ground. Treats culture as a pre-existing fact to be sorted.

### 3.2 Non-Western lineages that do it differently

- **Ifá (Yoruba, pre-colonial)** — 256 Odù. Each Odù is both sign and operation. A diviner casts the Odù; the verse *generates* the interpretation. This is operational-cosmological mapping in its pure form. Predates Leibniz's binary system by centuries.
- **I Ching (Chinese, Zhou dynasty)** — 64 hexagrams built from two primitives (yin/yang). Combinatorial and cosmological. The *Richard Wilhelm* translation introduced operational thinking to Western readers in the 20th c.
- **Kabbalah (Jewish esoteric)** — Tree of Life. Ten sephirot + twenty-two paths. Cosmological and generative (the *name* generates the world).
- **Tantric mapping (Hindu/Buddhist)** — chakras, yantras, mandalas. Diagram as generator of state.
- **Ifá's companion systems** — Bamana sand divination (Mali), geomancy, *raml* (Arabic), *merindinlogun* (16-cowrie). All operational-cosmological.
- **Akan adinkra symbols** — each carries philosophical weight. Not merely decorative; each is a generative concept (*sankofa*, *gye nyame*, *nkyinkyim*).
- **Dogon cosmology** — spiral-genesis cosmology (Ogotemmêli / Marcel Griaule). The universe as word-event (*nommo*).
- **Chinese five-elements (Wu Xing)** — operational cosmology. Elements generate and destroy one another; every phenomenon mapped through their interplay.
- **Islamic taxonomy of knowledge (al-Farabi, al-Ghazali)** — mapping sciences against theology and the seeker's journey. Not purely typological.

What all of these share: **meaning is generated by the system, not just sorted by it**. They are cosmological-operational. They do not classify existing things; they *produce* interpretations, diagnoses, states, outcomes.

### 3.3 The colonial erasure

Colonial knowledge-production flattened these systems into "folk beliefs" or "primitive classifications." Ifá became "superstition." Adinkra became "ornament." Kabbalah became "occultism." I Ching became an exotic curiosity.

What was lost: the recognition that these were **full-stack mapping methods**, equal to and often older than Linnaean typology, and structurally different. They were generative where Western taxonomy was descriptive. They were cosmological where Western taxonomy was flat. They grounded in practice where Western taxonomy claimed neutrality.

### 3.4 The decolonial recovery (20th–21st c.)

A partial list of recoveries:

- **Henry Louis Gates Jr.** — *The Signifying Monkey* (1988). Esu-Elegbara as theoretical figure; Yoruba semiotics as literary method.
- **Paul Gilroy** — *The Black Atlantic* (1993). Diasporic mapping as counter-culture of modernity.
- **Édouard Glissant** — *Poétique de la Relation* (1990). Mapping as relation-making, not sorting.
- **Wilson Harris** — decades of fiction and essay. The "quantum imagination" as operational mapping.
- **Kamau Brathwaite** — *History of the Voice* (1984). "Nation language" as recovered linguistic mapping.
- **Sylvia Wynter** — long-running critique of "the genre of Man." Mapping as ontology.
- **Kodwo Eshun** — *More Brilliant Than the Sun* (1998). Sonic fiction, hyperstition.
- **Achille Mbembe** — *Critique of Black Reason* (2013), *Out of the Dark Night* (2021). Afropolitan mapping.
- **Denise Ferreira da Silva** — fractal thinking; Black feminist poethics.

These recoveries do not just describe African or diasporic traditions. They *re-execute* them as contemporary method. They run the operational-cosmological mode inside contemporary critical practice.

### 3.5 The Middle-Passage-tested lineages

A separate class of operational-cosmological mapping systems deserves its own heading: the **diasporic branches** of Afro-Atlantic religion that were forced underground by slavery and colonial criminalization, survived in creolized form, and in several cases are among the richest operational-cosmological systems still actively practiced.

What distinguishes them from their source traditions is **condition of survival**. Each was suppressed, each encoded itself as embodied practice rather than text, and each absorbed material from the suppressing culture (Catholic saints, Kongo kimpasi, Bantu language, Amerindian spirits) into a functional syncretism — not dilution but *tactical absorption*. They are the cleanest proof that operational-cosmological mapping survives across ruptures.

- **Vodou (Haiti)** — Dahomey/Fon/Kongo fusion. Lwa divided into Rada (cool, inherited) and Petwo (hot, forged-in-Haiti) *nanchons*. **Vévé** — the ground-sigil, each specific to one Lwa — is a living designation-glyph system. The Haitian Revolution (1791–1804) was preceded by the Bois Caïman Vodou ceremony; the map *was* the insurgency.

- **Lucumí / Santería / Regla de Ocha (Cuba)** — Yoruba-derived. Orishas syncretized with Catholic saints (Shango / Santa Bárbara; Obatalá / Our Lady of Mercy). **Diloggún** divination — 16-cowrie shell system — operates on a smaller latent space than Ifá's 256 but the same logic. *Patakines* (teaching-stories) attached to each throw make the system operational-narrative at once.

- **Palo Monte / Palo Mayombe / Palo Briyumba / Palo Kimbisa (Cuba)** — Kongo-derived, operates independently of the Yoruba-Catholic syncretism. Core object is the **nganga** — a cauldron/vessel containing earth, bones, iron, charged to become operational. Palo is explicitly "hot" — more raw, more transactional, less mediated than Lucumí. The two traditions coexist in Cuba often in the same practitioner; they do different work.

- **Candomblé (Brazil)** — Yoruba (Ketu nation), Fon-Ewe (Jeje nation), and Bantu (Angola nation) traditions organized by **nação**. Orixás (Portuguese spelling) with specific axé (Yoruba *ashe*) characteristics. **Terreiro** as the sacred ground. Deep preservation of West African religious operation, especially Ketu, which retains more Yoruba-original material than modern Nigeria in some respects.

- **Umbanda (Brazil)** — syncretic later tradition (20th c.); blends Candomblé, Kardec-style spiritism, and Catholic elements. Operational-cosmological with a more mediumistic emphasis.

- **Winti (Suriname)** — Akan + Fon + Kongo-derived. "Wind spirits." Particularly resilient because the Saramaka and Ndyuka Maroons preserved it deep in the rainforest.

- **Obeah / Myalism (Jamaica, wider Anglophone Caribbean)** — Akan-derived (Ashanti) spiritual and healing complex. **Myal** is the healing/purifying side; *obeah* historically came to be stigmatized by colonial authorities (and criminalized — the Obeah Act in Jamaica was not fully repealed until 2013). Reclaiming the word is itself a decolonial move.

- **Kumina (Jamaica)** — Kongo-derived drumming and spirit-possession tradition, preserved particularly in St. Thomas parish. Operational in the most direct sense: the drums call specific ancestors.

- **Revival / Revival Zion / Pocomania (Jamaica)** — Afro-Christian syncretic movements. Trance, baptism, collective spirit-work.

- **Rastafari (Jamaica → global, 20th c.)** — *This is the anomaly*. Unlike the older traditions, Rastafari coined much of its vocabulary in the 20th century, in real time, explicitly at the colonial rupture. **Livity** (way of living), **overstand** (vs understand), **I-and-I** (plural/communal self), **Babylon** (coined as oppressor-system), **Zion** (return-place), **Jah** (the divine), **Chant down Babylon** (hyperstition as doctrine: wordsound have power). Rastafari is the proof that the method can be *fresh-coined* and still carry immediately. It is Pan-African hyperstition in practice.

- **Maroon societies (Jamaica, Suriname, Brazil, Colombia, Haiti, Guyana)** — parallel to the religious traditions, the Maroons established *free territories* inside the plantation colonies. Jamaican Maroons (Trelawny Town, Accompong, Moore Town), Saramaka and Ndyuka of Suriname, Palmares in Brazil (led by Zumbi), Palenque de San Basilio in Colombia (still speaks Palenquero Kreyòl). The **abeng** — cow-horn — was the Maroons' long-distance communication system, its call patterns carrying across mountain terrain. **Marronage** is itself an operation: the act of escape plus the establishment of a counter-society on terrain the colonial map cannot reach.

Each of these systems encodes a full operational-cosmological map. Each survived conditions designed to extinguish it. Each is a *living* proof that the mapping compound is robust — that when the Western descriptive map attempts to overwrite, the deeper map simply encodes itself in ritual, dance, drum, vévé, nganga, chant, and goes on operating.

### 3.6 Kemet as the oldest documented compound

One tradition deserves special attention because it **already named the compound we are calling Boveda mapping**.

Heliopolitan theology (Old Kingdom Egypt, ~2500 BCE; but the thought is older) explicitly theorized creation as the combination of three divine principles:

- **Sia** (*sjꜣ*) — perception, the seeing of what already is, the diviner's sight.
- **Hu** (*ḥw*) — authoritative utterance, the first commanding word, Atum's breath.
- **Heka** (*ḥkꜣ*) — activated magic, the force that makes the utterance real, the energy of manifestation.

*Sia perceives the order. Hu names it. Heka makes it so.*

This triad is structurally identical to the compound this book argues for:
- **Sia ≈ cosmological substrate** (perceiving what already is)
- **Hu ≈ mythopoetic authorship** (utterance)
- **Heka ≈ operational generation** (activation)

**Kemet named the compound four and a half millennia ago.** Western critical theory is late to the question. The word *heka* in Egyptian is not "magic" in the disenchanted modern sense; it is the *energy of effective speech*, the force by which language bends reality. Every ritual utterance by a Kemetic priest was a *heka-act*: the words did something, they did not describe.

The same tradition distinguished two kinds of eternity:
- **Neheh** (*nḥḥ*) — cyclic eternity; recurrence; the sun returning each day.
- **Djet** (*ḏt*) — static eternity; completion; the unchanging; the preserved.

A real thing exists simultaneously in both. Every god, every sacred object, every pyramid is *at once* Neheh and Djet — returning and fixed. This is the classical Mediterranean problem of "squaring the circle" — reconciling the grid with the cycle — **theologically resolved in the Nile Valley long before it was posed in Athens**.

The geometric form of a successful squaring is the rose: a circle made of finite petals. The cosmic rose of Dante's *Paradiso* (itself influenced by Neoplatonic transmission from Alexandria), the rose-windows of medieval cathedrals, the Sufi rose, the compass rose. **In Boveda mapping terms: every canon work is a petal, eras are the radii, arcs are the concentric rings, designations are the angles of entry. The body of work is the rose.**

Other Kemetic primitives worth carrying:
- **Ma'at** — cosmic order, truth, balance (personified as goddess with a feather)
- **Ka** — vital essence, the double
- **Ba** — personality, soul-that-flies-at-night
- **Ren** — true name; knowing a thing's Ren is having power over it (the metaphysical justification for naming-at-bridges)
- **Sekhem** — power, vital force (analogous to Yoruba *ashe*)
- **Akh** — the transfigured spirit
- **Djed** — the backbone-pillar of Osiris; stability (architectural primitive)
- **Benu** — phoenix; return through destruction; Neheh embodied
- **Atum** — self-creating creator-god; creation from nothing

The takeaway for method: **Sia-Hu-Heka is the citation-ready name for the compound.** When someone asks what Boveda mapping is structurally, the crispest answer is: *a Sia-Hu-Heka method — perceiving the substrate, uttering the names, activating through operation.* Kemet gave us the word before Western critical theory posed the question. We take the word back.

### 3.7 Igbo and Ijaw — the speaker's primary ground

The speaker of this book (Cherubsphinx) stands at a specific four-way crossroads. Two of those four are Niger Delta cosmologies — **Igbo** (southeastern Nigeria) and **Ijaw** (the riverine Delta proper). These are the *primary* substrates of the method. Where the earlier sections document the broad Afro-Atlantic traditions the method borrows from, this section documents the specific cosmologies the method was *formed inside*. They are not interchangeable with Yoruba/Ifá, which is a close West African neighbour but a distinct system.

#### Igbo — *Odinani* and its primitives

The Igbo religious-philosophical system as a whole is called **Odinani** (or *Odinala*) — literally "that of the land." Unlike some West African systems, Odinani is decentralized: there is no single priestly hierarchy, no one sacred text; it lives in household altars, community masquerades, proverbs, divination, and the ethics of *nso ala* (abomination against the land).

Core primitives:

- **Chi** — the personal destiny-spirit; every person has their own *chi*, no two are alike. Achebe: *"A man's chi is said to walk behind him."* It is not fate in the Greek sense (*moira*) but a relational companion-force. When a person is in right alignment with their chi, they act with *ike* (power); when out of alignment, no external effort will suffice. Chi maps to what modern Western discourse calls *vocation* or *calling* — but more active, more agential.
- **Chukwu / Chineke** — the supreme God; *Chukwu* = "great chi"; *Chineke* = "the chi that creates." The transcendent source; relatively distant from daily practice (which happens through the *alusi* and ancestors).
- **Alusi / Arusi** — the tutelary deities, tied to specific functions or places (*Ala* the earth, *Amadioha* the thunder-sky, *Idemili* the river, *Agwu* the healer-patron). Each *alusi* is both a cosmic force and a household / village presence.
- **Ala / Ani** — the earth-mother; morality principle; *nso ala* = abomination against the land, the gravest wrong. In Odinani, ethics is not commandment-based but earth-based: violate the land and the land itself withdraws.
- **Ogbanje / Ọgbanje** — the "repeater" child, a spirit who dies young and returns to the same mother to be born again, often multiple times in one lifetime. *Ogbanje* operates on explicit cyclic-return logic. Akwaeke Emezi's *Freshwater* (2018) is the clearest contemporary *ogbanje*-cosmology-operationalized-in-fiction; Achebe's *Things Fall Apart* and *Ahiara* lectures also map the concept rigorously. For Boveda mapping, *ogbanje* is a direct primitive for the return-of-an-era, the same work arriving in a new body.
- **Afa** — the Igbo divination system. Like Ifá, it uses a chain/cord with binary-position beads; the diviner (*dibia*) casts and reads the configuration. Afa is cousin-to-Ifá across the Niger but distinct in its algebra and interpretive corpus. Primary source: Patrick Iroegbu's studies of Afa divination.
- **Dibia** — the healer-diviner-seer. A *dibia afa* reads the divination; a *dibia ogwu* works with herbs and medicine. The role is operational, not hereditary-priestly.
- **Mmanwu / Mmonwu** — masquerade; the ancestor or spirit takes bodily form in the masked figure. Cognate with Yoruba *Egungun*. Each masquerade has its own name, its own season, its own rhythmic identity.
- **Ikenga** — the horned figure (carved wooden statuette or communal altar) representing achievement, the right hand, power-through-action. Every man of substance carries one. Distinctly Igbo; has no direct Yoruba or Vodun equivalent.
- **Ofo** — the staff of truth and justice; held by a titled elder; invocation of the *ofo* compels honesty.
- **Ndichie** — the ancestors; those who have gone before and continue to witness. Libation and sacrifice keep the *ndichie* present; neglect severs the lineage.

#### Ijaw — the Niger Delta riverine cosmology

The Ijaw (Izon) people live in the Niger Delta proper — the mangrove-and-creek ecosystem where the Niger meets the Atlantic. Their cosmology is shaped by water the way highland cosmologies are shaped by mountain: boat is sacred vessel, creek is threshold, mangrove is liminal edge. The sub-groups include Kalabari (Elem Kalabari), Nembe, Brass, Bonny, Okrika — each with distinct variations but shared core.

Core primitives:

- **Egbesu** — the god of righteous warfare and justice. *Egbesu* protects those fighting in a cause recognized as just; those without the justice do not receive the protection. Central to 20th-century Niger Delta resistance (Isaac Adaka Boro, MEND, the Ijaw Youth Council). A primitive of principled force.
- **Mami Wata** — the pan-Niger-Delta water-spirit; mermaid-figure of wealth, beauty, danger, esoteric knowledge. She is present across the entire Atlantic coast of Africa (Igbo, Efik, Yoruba, coastal Cameroon, to Ghana) but has particular Ijaw weight. Mami Wata is not a single goddess but a *class* of water-spirits — pan-African oceanic consciousness. She crosses the Atlantic into Haitian *La Sirène*, Cuban *Yemayá/Yemoja* (Yoruba-sourced), Brazilian *Iemanjá*.
- **Ekine** — the Kalabari water-spirit masquerade society. The *Ekine* members dance specific water spirits (called *owuamapu*); each spirit has its own drum pattern, its own dance-step, its own mask. The Kalabari historian Robin Horton's foundational studies (*Kalabari Ekine Society*, 1963) are the key English-language source. Ekine is operational-cosmological in its purest ritual form: the dance *is* the spirit appearing.
- **Duein fubara** — the Kalabari ancestral screen. Carved wooden panels, often large, housing the spirit of a named ancestor (usually a king, *amanyanabo*). Each screen is a specific person's presence-vessel. The British Museum and the Boston Museum of Fine Arts hold significant collections — much of it stolen; repatriation conversations ongoing.
- **Benikurukuru** (or regional variants) — the creator in some Ijaw traditions.
- **Ọmịnị** — "one from the water"; a class of water-spirit that appears as a beautiful human and carries persons between worlds.
- **Amakiri / Amatu** — founder-ancestor figures of specific Kalabari houses.
- **Sibari / Ziba** — ancestor reverence complex; libation-based.

The riverine structure of Ijaw cosmology matters methodologically: threshold-figures are water-figures; passage-rituals are boat-rituals; the mangrove-edge is liminal in the same way a Yoruba *orita* (crossroads) is liminal. For Boveda mapping inside Igbo-Ijaw heritage, the relevant images are **the creek as threshold** and **the water-spirit as crossing-figure** — Ekine dance invoking Mami Wata, the boat carrying the shore-being across.

#### Literary operationalization

Writers who operationalized Igbo and Ijaw cosmology in contemporary literary form:

**Igbo:**
- Chinua Achebe — *Things Fall Apart* (1958), *Arrow of God* (1964), *No Longer at Ease* (1960). Achebe rendered *ofo*, *chi*, *alusi*, *ogbanje* inside the English-language novel without italicizing, forcing the Western reader to enter the cosmology on its terms.
- Flora Nwapa — *Efuru* (1966), *Idu* (1970). Oguta Lake cosmology; the water-goddess *Uhamiri* as central figure. First African woman novelist.
- Chukwuemeka Ike — *The Potter's Wheel* (1973).
- Akwaeke Emezi — *Freshwater* (2018), *The Death of Vivek Oji* (2020), *Pet* (2019). *Freshwater* is the single most rigorous contemporary *ogbanje*-cosmology-as-autobiography in the English-language literary record. An explicit Boveda-mapped object.
- Chimamanda Ngozi Adichie — *Purple Hibiscus*, *Half of a Yellow Sun*, *Americanah*.
- Chika Unigwe — *On Black Sisters Street*, *Night Dancer*.
- Helon Habila — *Oil on Water*, *Travellers*.
- Chinweizu (with Jemie and Madubuike) — *Toward the Decolonization of African Literature* (1980); *Decolonising the African Mind* (1987). Theoretical / critical work on Igbo + Pan-African aesthetics.

**Ijaw / Niger Delta:**
- Gabriel Okara — *The Voice* (1964). Okara translated Ijaw syntax and rhythm *directly into English*, refusing to naturalize. Result: a novel that reads "wrong" to Standard-English expectation but carries Ijaw thought-shape. A foundational rupture-point coinage.
- J.P. Clark-Bekederemo — *Song of a Goat* (1961), *The Ozidi Saga* (1977). Ozidi is the great Ijaw epic, a multi-day recited narrative of a hero's return; Clark's verse-play adaptation and scholarly recording of the oral version are both canonical.
- Isaac Adaka Boro — *The Twelve-Day Revolution* (1982). Ijaw nationalist leader; operational-political writing.
- Tanure Ojaide — Urhobo (cultural neighbour to Ijaw), Niger Delta poet.
- Ken Saro-Wiwa — Ogoni, also Niger Delta context; *Sozaboy* (1985), *A Month and a Day* (1995).

These writers — especially Emezi, Okara, and Achebe — model the specific method this book argues for: **operationalize the cosmology inside the contemporary form, refuse translation-as-obligation, let the reader enter on the cosmology's terms**. They are the proximate literary precedents for anyone doing Boveda mapping from Igbo + Ijaw ground.

## 4. The compound: mythopoetic-operational on cosmological ground

The specific compound this research calls **Boveda mapping** is three modes at once:

1. **Cosmological ground.** Rooted in inherited substrate (e.g., Ifá + Akan + Yoruba, or any specific tradition the creator actually stands in). Non-negotiable. Without it, the mapping floats.
2. **Mythopoetic authorship.** The creator names what is not yet named. Coins eras, designations, forms, operations. Without this, the mapping is museum-work.
3. **Operational generation.** Primitives combine via named operations to produce works. Without this, the mapping is decorative.

The compound is not a 2-of-3. All three are required. Each shores up the others' failure modes:

- Cosmological alone → ethnography.
- Mythopoetic alone → fantasy without weight.
- Operational alone → algorithm without world.
- Cosmological + mythopoetic (no operations) → mystical aphorism.
- Cosmological + operational (no mythopoetic) → divinatory system without new works.
- Mythopoetic + operational (no cosmological) → science fiction without roots.

Only the full compound produces a **usable, rooted, generative** system.

## 5. Precedent studies

Close readings of thinkers/artists who execute the compound. Not exhaustive. Each one models a different emphasis.

### 5.1 Wilson Harris — the quantum imagination

Books: *Palace of the Peacock* (1960), *The Whole Armour*, *The Guyana Quartet*, *The Infinite Rehearsal* (1987), *The Mask of the Beggar* (2003). Essays: *Tradition, the Writer and Society*, *The Womb of Space*.

**Cosmological ground.** Guyana's triple inheritance — indigenous (Arawak, Carib), African (via the slave trade), European (Dutch, British). Also Hindu (indentured labour) and Chinese. Harris refuses to let any single tradition dominate.

**Mythopoetic authorship.** The river becomes Dante's underworld. The ghosts are the indigenous murdered. The European classical inheritance is absorbed into the Guyanese landscape rather than the reverse. Harris invents a vocabulary: "carnival," "quantum imagination," "alchemy of the imagination," "cross-cultural fabric."

**Operational engine.** The "quantum imagination" is Harris's named operation: the ability to hold multiple incompatible realities simultaneously without resolving them into synthesis. Every novel performs it. *Palace of the Peacock* rewrites its own deaths in successive chapters. The operation is: *let reality superpose; refuse the resolving move*.

**What to borrow.** The refusal of purity. The operation (quantum imagination / superposition). The confidence to treat Dante as raw material equal to Arawak cosmology.

### 5.2 Édouard Glissant — the poetics of relation

Books: *Le Discours antillais* (1981), *Poétique de la Relation* (1990), *Faulkner, Mississippi* (1996), *Traité du Tout-Monde* (1997).

**Cosmological ground.** Caribbean creolized world; the Plantation as origin-site.

**Mythopoetic authorship.** Glissant's coinages are among the most citable in modern thought: *opacity*, *errance* (erring/wandering), *creolization*, *relation*, *Tout-Monde*, *antillanité*. Each is a precisely defined primitive.

**Operational engine.** The poetics of Relation is a method. Creolization is an operation: the unpredictable encounter between two or more cultural matter that produces something irreducible to either. Opacity is a defensive operation: the right to remain un-translated. Errance is a movement-operation: wandering without origin-nostalgia.

**What to borrow.** The naming discipline. The explicit coinage of every key term. The articulation of operations as much as ontology.

### 5.3 Arvo Pärt — tintinnabuli

Works: *Tabula Rasa* (1977), *Cantus in Memoriam Benjamin Britten* (1977), *Spiegel im Spiegel* (1978), *Passio* (1982), *Miserere* (1989), *Kanon Pokajanen* (1997).

**Cosmological ground.** Orthodox Christian liturgy. Centuries of chant. Not invented; inherited.

**Mythopoetic authorship.** Pärt names his method *tintinnabuli* (from Latin *tintinnabulum*, "little bell"). This is the move. The method has a proper noun. He does not just compose in a style; he announces a method.

**Operational engine.** Two voices, M and T. M is the melodic voice, moving stepwise. T is the tintinnabulation voice, arpeggiating a triad (usually tonic). Every pitch in T is determined by a strict rule relative to M. The compositional act is: choose the rule, let the rule produce the piece. It is algebraic. It is operational.

**What to borrow.** The discipline of a named method. The willingness to work *inside* a single rule-system across a lifetime of work. The simplicity: two voices, one rule, unlimited output.

### 5.4 Sun Ra — Saturn cosmology and Arkestra

Recordings: *Jazz in Silhouette* (1959), *The Heliocentric Worlds of Sun Ra* (1965), *Space Is the Place* (1973), hundreds of records.

**Cosmological ground.** Composite: Egyptian esotericism (Alice and George G. M. James, Mystery Schools), biblical apocalyptic, Chicago bebop, African diasporic memory.

**Mythopoetic authorship.** Sun Ra's signature move is that he scripted his origin. He claimed to be from Saturn. He refused his birth name (Herman Blount). He composed a genealogy — the Arkestra, the Heliocentric Worlds, the concept of cosmic blackness. He lived the myth as autobiography. When interviewers asked for facts, he answered with cosmology. He never broke character because there was no character outside the cosmology.

**Operational engine.** Modal + harmolodic + free improvisation operating over a rhythmic scaffold. The "Arkestra discipline" was a practice: daily rehearsal, shared meals, cosmological instruction. The band was a ritual operation as much as a performance unit.

**What to borrow.** The authorship of origin. The refusal to separate art from cosmology. The willingness to have the method be lived, not just applied.

### 5.5 Ursula K. Le Guin — carrier bag theory

Books: *A Wizard of Earthsea* (1968), *The Left Hand of Darkness* (1969), *The Dispossessed* (1974), *Always Coming Home* (1985). Essays: *The Carrier Bag Theory of Fiction* (1986), *Dancing at the Edge of the World* (1989).

**Cosmological ground.** Taoism (her father Alfred Kroeber translated the *Tao Te Ching*). Anthropology. Her own mythopoetic readings of California indigenous cosmologies in *Always Coming Home*.

**Mythopoetic authorship.** Earthsea's true-name magic. Gethen's gender-fluid humans. The anarchist physics of *The Dispossessed*. Each novel is a world, not a setting.

**Operational engine.** *Carrier Bag Theory of Fiction*: a method-essay. Le Guin argues that the first human tool was not the spear (hero narrative) but the carrier bag (gatherer narrative). Stories should be carrier bags: they collect, hold, carry; they do not drive toward a conquest climax. This is an operational rubric. Applied consistently, it produces a specific kind of work.

**What to borrow.** The method-essay. The willingness to articulate *how* you work, in writing, as a durable rubric separate from the work itself.

### 5.6 Octavia Butler — personal craft rules as algorithm

Books: *Kindred* (1979), *Wild Seed* (1980), the *Xenogenesis* trilogy (1987–89), *Parable of the Sower* (1993), *Parable of the Talents* (1998).

**Cosmological ground.** Black feminist cosmology. Diasporic memory. Biological kinship reimagined through alien-symbiote metaphor.

**Mythopoetic authorship.** Whole cosmologies built per series. The Oankali. The Doro bloodline. Earthseed theology.

**Operational engine.** Butler famously kept and published her craft-rules as personal instruction: *So be it. See to it.* Her affirmations were operational. Her drafting principles (persist; study; imagine difference; do not look away from pain) functioned as an algorithm for producing work across decades.

**What to borrow.** Personal craft-rules as an explicit layer of the method. Affirmations as operations (ritual-performative at the level of the maker themselves).

### 5.7 Kodwo Eshun — sonic fiction, hyperstition

Book: *More Brilliant Than the Sun: Adventures in Sonic Fiction* (1998). Essays and lectures on Afrofuturism.

**Cosmological ground.** Black Atlantic (extending Gilroy). Afrofuturism as a stance rooted in displacement.

**Mythopoetic authorship.** Eshun writes music criticism *as* sonic fiction. His criticism does not explain the music; it extends it into prose. He coins: *sonic fiction*, *hyperstition* (a fiction that, by being believed, makes itself real), *more brilliant than the sun* (afrofuturism as celestial operation).

**Operational engine.** Hyperstition is an operation: *write the future you want to inhabit until it begins to come true*. It is performative. It is cosmological. It is scalable across practices (Detroit techno hyperstitions its own future-retrofit machinery; Sun Ra hyperstitioned Saturn).

**What to borrow.** Hyperstition as a generative move. The recognition that criticism can be a continuation of art rather than its analysis.

### 5.8 Donna Haraway — sympoiesis

Books: *Simians, Cyborgs, and Women* (1991), *When Species Meet* (2008), *Staying with the Trouble* (2016).

**Cosmological ground.** Feminist science studies, multispecies thinking, biology as a cosmology.

**Mythopoetic authorship.** *Cyborg Manifesto* is a myth in manifesto form. *Chthulucene* replaces *Anthropocene*. *Sympoiesis* (making-with) replaces autopoiesis (self-making). The Cthulhu tentacular stands in for a different cosmology than the Enlightenment subject.

**Operational engine.** Sympoiesis: operations are always relational. No self-making. Every making is making-with. "Staying with the trouble" is an explicit operational slogan — do not resolve, do not escape; stay, compose, respond.

**What to borrow.** Operations-as-slogans. The willingness to write theory at the register of a usable rule.

### 5.9 Other worth-studying

- **Fela Kuti** — afrobeat as full-stack method (musical, political, cosmological).
- **Abdullah Ibrahim** — Cape Town township harmonics + Ellington + Sufi devotion.
- **Alice Coltrane** — spiritual jazz into bhajan. Cross-tradition devotional mapping.
- **Ben Okri** — *The Famished Road* operates Yoruba abiku-spirit cosmology inside the novel form.
- **Nommo theorists (Dogon studies)** — Ogotemmêli, Griaule, Dieterlen. Full-stack cosmological mapping.
- **John Akomfrah** — triptych/multichannel archive-as-form.
- **Toni Morrison** — oral call-response inside European novel structure.
- **Kehinde Wiley / Amoako Boafo** — classical portraiture operated on Black subjects as method, not just content.
- **Theaster Gates** — ritual-operational mapping in sculpture and social practice.

## 6. How to build a Boveda mapping of your own

A procedural guide. Not prescriptive on *content*, only on *method*.

### Step 1. Name your substrate honestly.

Which inherited cosmology do you actually stand in? Not which one sounds good. Which one were you formed by? If you are diasporic, your substrate is *the specific creolization* you were born into (e.g., "Nigerian-raised, British-educated, hip-hop-formed") — that is not a fracture, it is your substrate. Write it down. Include the fissures.

### Step 2. Inherit primitives. Do not invent what exists.

Go deep on your substrate. Learn the real terms. *Ashe*. *Nommo*. *Sankofa*. *Ubuntu*. *Irony* (as Ndebele uses it). Do not paraphrase them into English; let them keep their body.

### Step 3. Identify your ruptures.

Where do your inheritances collide? European classical against African rhythm? Street vernacular against academic critique? Quantum physics against ancestral return? Those are your bridges. Each bridge is a site where new primitives need to be coined.

### Step 4. Coin at the bridges.

*Badboy Klavier*. *Schism-Bach*. *Violet Igbodu*. The name performs the rupture. Resist the temptation to coin everywhere; the coinage must be earned by a real bridge.

### Step 5. Name your operations.

What do you *do* to your primitives? Combine? Oppose? Invert? Invoke? Return? Name each operation. Write each down as a method-entry. Pärt's *tintinnabuli* is one operation; he built forty years of work on it.

### Step 6. Declare your lineage dually.

Every work should declare two lineages: *technical* (the tools, forms, techniques you inherited) and *cultural* (the ancestors, traditions, stances). Do not let either hide.

### Step 7. Decide what is canon.

Canon is not a release state. It is a curatorial decision: *this belongs to the real body of work*. Canonize deliberately. Demote willingly. Some works are sandbox forever; that is honest.

### Step 8. Name the method itself.

Without a proper name, a method cannot be cited, extended, or inherited. Boveda mapping. Violet Grammar. Hollow Form. Pick one. Live it long enough that readers learn it.

### Step 9. Refuse what the method refuses.

When a decision looks like Western descriptive-typology, refuse it. When it looks like mythopoetic-operational-cosmological authorship, accept it. Hold the line.

## 8. Open questions / lines of further research

1. **How does Boveda mapping scale to collaboration?** Every precedent above was largely individual (or a single named collective like the Arkestra). Can the method survive in looser collectives, open-source projects, crowd-authored worlds?

2. **What is the relationship between Boveda mapping and machine learning?** Embeddings, operations on latent space, generative models — these are all operational-generative by structure. Can LLM/diffusion systems be *grounded* in a specific cosmology rather than trained on an indiscriminate scrape?

3. **Which cosmologies reject mapping entirely?** Some traditions (certain Zen and mystic apophatic practices) reject systematic mapping as such. Is that a refusal of the compound, or another leaf of it?

4. **What happens in the second generation?** Pärt's tintinnabuli is now taught. Inheritors work inside it. Does the method retain authorship-vitality, or calcify into style?

5. **Tidalectic reception — can it be systematized?** Brathwaite's call-response model. Can the "between-works" layer be modelled formally without collapsing into pure graph-theory?

6. **The post-canonical question.** What happens to Boveda mapping when the body of work is "complete"? Does the cosmology keep generating, or does the method become historical?

7. **The ethics of invention.** When does coining-at-bridges tip into appropriation? Rule-of-thumb answers exist; a precise one does not.
