## What PLATS-Bioacoustic is

PLATS is the writer's latent-cartography movement: mapping diffusion
latent space and binding regions to odu ciphers (the writer's term
is *odugraphy*). PLATS-Bioacoustic is the natural-world wing. It
extends latent cartography from the synthetic image manifold into
the living manifold: species, soundscapes, ecologies, surveying
methods, the actual measurable strange of the world.

Where PLATS-original asks "where in latent space does Eden live",
PLATS-Bioacoustic asks "where in living space does Eden live, and
what does it sound like, and what is being lost there right now".

Both are cartography. Both refuse the colonial map's flattening.
Both make the cipher the unit of meaning, not the pixel or the
coordinate.

## The frame, one sentence

A method for mapping the natural world's signals as latent regions
that can be heard, seen, navigated, and quoted — the same
discipline a poet brings to a line, applied to a coral reef.

## The frame, one paragraph

PLATS-Bioacoustic treats every living signal as cartographic.
A coral reef's chorus is a map. A pulsar's timing is a map. An
elephant's infrasonic rumble through dirt is a map. We capture
those maps with the right instruments, render them as Relics,
sonify and visualise them as legible artefacts, and bind them into
mythic worlds so the data never floats free of stake. The
discipline is queer ecology with field tools; the posture is
Discovery Channel without the colonial gaze; the lineage runs
from Ken Saro-Wiwa through Donna Haraway through the writer's
father's surveyor's notebook.

## The six-layer stack

This is the structural argument:

1. **Field signal** — what is alive and signalling: forest, lake, reef, wetland, city, cave, archive, animal, machine, body.
2. **Scientific capture** — the instruments: hydrophones, contact mics, geophones, ultrasonic mics, field recorders, eDNA, LiDAR, satellite imagery, thermal imaging, drone photogrammetry.
3. **Intelligence** — what we know about the signal: species ID, anomaly detection, acoustic indices, ecological metadata, GIS location, time, weather, migration patterns.
4. **Sonification** — the translation: ecological variables to rhythm, harmony, texture, modulation, spatial audio, feedback systems.
5. **Visualisation** — the seeing: GIS maps, spectral waterfalls, 3D landscapes, point clouds, neural maps, Unreal / TouchDesigner ecosystems.
6. **Mythic / art** — the binding: queer ecology, African futurism, gothic natural history, non-human ritual, the club system that lets you dance to what the elephants heard.

Each layer is its own discipline. PLATS-Bioacoustic argues they
have to be walked together. A spectrogram without a mythic posture
is taxidermy. A mythic posture without the spectrogram is
decoration. The article should hold both at the same time.

## The seed phenomena, one paragraph each

### Infrasonic elephant communication

Elephants make long, low rumbles between 5 and 35 Hz that travel
both through the air and, more powerfully, through the ground.
A rumble can carry ten kilometres or more, coupled into the soil
as a seismic wave. Karen McComb and Joyce Poole catalogued the
vocal repertoire; Caitlin O'Connell-Rodwell showed elephants
detect ground signals through pressure-sensitive cells in their
feet and trunk tip. This is communication below human hearing,
through dirt. Capture it with a geophone; play it back at speed
and it becomes a register the writer's own work can sit inside.

### Seismic substrate transmission

The whole world of animal communication via dirt. Karen
Warkentin showed red-eyed tree frog embryos hatch early when they
feel the wasp's vibrational signature on the leaf. Peggy Hill
documents insect substrate signalling across orders — leafhoppers,
stink bugs, treehoppers — all "talking" through plant stems and
soil. This is a queering of acoustic ecology: most communication
is not airborne. Most communication is not human-audible. Most
communication is below.

### Whale calls

Sperm whale codas are clan dialects. Hal Whitehead spent forty
years showing cetaceans carry culture, with each sperm whale
matrilineal clan having its own click rhythm. Humpback song
travels ocean basins and updates yearly. Christopher Clark at
Cornell's BRP records blue whale calls that span hundreds of
miles. Hydrophones catch this. Time-extension brings it into
human range. The mythic move: clan codas as ciphers. Each clan
has its own odu.

### Coral reef acoustic signatures

A healthy reef sounds like a kitchen at dinnertime. Steve Simpson
(Exeter) and Tim Lamont (Lancaster) showed bleached reefs go
silent — the snapping shrimp, the territorial fish, the
substrate-grazing parrotfish, all gone. The reef's audio becomes
a diagnostic for its health. The article should sit with this: we
can hear ecosystems dying.

### Volcanic tremor

The USGS Hawaiian Volcano Observatory and IRIS run broadband
seismometers that catch tremor between 0.01 and 100 Hz. Sustained
harmonic tremor predicts eruption. At one hundred times normal
speed, tremor becomes a drone instrument with strict physical
provenance. Mount Yasur's basso continuo. The writer's PERI patch
already speaks this register.

### Glacial cracking

EastGRIP and the Drift+Noise pipeline put cryoseismometers and
contact mics into Greenland ice. Every crack is a calving event
measured to the second. The audio is climate evidence in real
time. Music that is also receipt.

### Bat echolocation

Bats call between 20 and 200 kHz, well above human hearing.
Pipistrelles peak at 45 kHz; horseshoe bats sit higher. BatDetect2
and Tadarida identify species by call pattern. Time-expand the
recording 10x and the rainforest at dusk becomes a chorus you
already knew was there. This is the simplest queer-ecological
intervention: the unheard becomes heard.

### Soil microbiome data

The EarthMicrobiome Project sequenced soils across the globe.
QIIME2 and MGnify make the data legible. A teaspoon of forest
soil contains more species than the visible animals of an
ecoregion. Sonifying microbial community diversity is sonifying
the most species-dense system on the planet. The article should
name how absurd it is that we still call this "dirt".

### eDNA traces

Filter ten litres of pond water through a Sterivex; sequence the
captured DNA; learn what fish, amphibians, mammals passed through.
The Cornell eDNA Initiative and NatureMetrics ship the protocols.
The "ghost census": detect without seeing. A queer-ecological
gesture by definition — the present-but-invisible counts.

### Astronomical sonification

Pulsar timing arrays produce ticks more regular than atomic
clocks. LIGO's gravitational-wave chirps are public domain. NASA
heliophysics publishes solar wind data. The Sun has a sound.
Saturn's magnetosphere has a song. The article should pull this
back from gimmick toward the real epistemological move: data
sonification is a way of knowing, not an aesthetic gloss.

## The two axes: strangeness + saro_index

Every Relic carries two numbers between 0 and 1. They are not the
same. The article should pair them as twin lenses.

- **strangeness** — how queer / cutting-edge / unique the
  phenomenon is. A common pigeon scores low. A slime mould solving
  the Tokyo subway scores high. An intrasonic-elephant-near-a-
  black-hole scores 1.0.
- **saro_index** — environmental-justice salience. Named for Ken
  Saro-Wiwa, the Ogoni writer executed by the Abacha regime in
  1995 for organising against Shell's destruction of the Niger
  Delta. A pristine glacier lake nobody is destroying scores low
  on saro. A mangrove kill site in Bonny scores 1.0.

The two axes generate four quadrants:

- **High strangeness, high saro** — the unique-and-dying. The
  vaquita porpoise. Highest urgency in the article. These Relics
  carry both the writer's wonder and the writer's grief.
- **High strangeness, low saro** — the unique-and-stable. Slime
  moulds. Pulsars. Wonder without immediate harm. Where joy lives.
- **Low strangeness, high saro** — the common-and-dying. House
  sparrows. Common birds in pesticide-drenched farmland.
  Heartbreak-of-the-everyday.
- **Low strangeness, low saro** — Background catalogue. Reference
  for the others.

The article should hold all four quadrants visible at once. PLATS-
Bioacoustic refuses to choose between wonder and witness.

## The Discovery Channel posture, translated

The user named it "Discovery Channel meets club system". This is
the affective register. Translate:

- **Wonder without sanitising.** Show the fungus eating the
  carpenter ant. Don't cut the tongue-eating louse.
- **Specificity over genre.** Not "rainforest" — "the Sundaland
  lowland forest of Kalimantan during the 1998 fire year".
- **Voice that holds knowledge without flexing it.** Attenborough's
  restraint. Saro-Wiwa's testimony. Kimmerer's reciprocity. Not
  National Geographic's exoticising gaze.
- **The strange and the political in one frame.** A radio-tagged
  bonobo IS the politics of bonobo conservation IS the queer
  ecology of female-coalition primates. Don't pick one. Hold all
  three at once.
- **The club system.** The dance floor as a venue for serious
  knowledge. The same energy that fills a club at 3am should fill
  the Listening Room scrolling a pulsar's timing track over a
  field recording of an Amazon rainstorm. Knowledge is sweat.
